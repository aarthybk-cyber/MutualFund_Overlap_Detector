================================================================================
MUTUAL FUND OVERLAP DETECTOR - PROJECT OVERVIEW
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THIS PROJECT IS FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Many investors hold several mutual funds believing this spreads their risk.
In practice, two differently-named funds -- even from different fund houses --
often own many of the same underlying stocks. If a big holding in that shared
cluster falls, it can drag down several "different" funds in the portfolio at
once, even though nothing on a standard fund fact sheet shows this.

The Mutual Fund Overlap Detector is a web app that answers two questions for
an investor:

  1. HOW MUCH DO TWO FUNDS OVERLAP?
     One overlap percentage between any pair of funds, based on their actual
     stock-level holdings for a given month.

  2. WHAT DO I REALLY OWN?
     If I put money into several funds, what is my true combined exposure to
     each underlying company, once the fund "wrappers" are removed?

This is the same look-through check a financial advisor would otherwise do by
hand from monthly disclosure PDFs. The app automates it across a full universe
of funds and three years of history.

IMPORTANT: This is an educational project built on synthetic (fictional) data.
It is not investment advice, and no output should be read as a buy, sell, or
hold recommendation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHO IT'S FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - An investor who holds multiple mutual funds and wants to check whether
    they are genuinely diversified or unintentionally concentrated.
  - A student or analyst learning how fund overlap and look-through exposure
    are actually calculated, with a real, working, adjustable tool.
  - Anyone curious how much two specific funds resemble each other.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. PROJECT PLAN (THREE PHASES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PHASE 1 - DATASET AND INGESTION  (Days 1-3)
  Goal: produce one single, clean, shared holdings dataset used by every
  later phase.
  Steps:
    1.1  Build a 350-stock universe (ISIN, company name, sector, cap rank)
    1.2  Define 60 fictional mutual funds across 7 categories/mandates
         (Large Cap, Mid Cap, Small Cap, Flexi Cap, ELSS, Index, Focused)
    1.3  Generate realistic month-end holding weights per fund, following
         each fund's mandate (e.g. large-cap funds lean toward the biggest
         companies, small-cap funds toward smaller ones)
    1.4  Extend holdings across 36 month-end dates (Jan 2023 - Dec 2025),
         with realistic month-to-month drift
    1.5  Simulate messy, disclosure-style data internally (inconsistent
         names, junk rows) to stress-test the cleaning step
    1.6  Clean the dataset using ISIN as the matching key (never company
         name, since the same company can be written several different ways)
    1.7  Assemble the final shared dataset: date, fund_name, amc, category,
         isin, company, sector, pct_nav, market_value
    1.8  Run a GO/NO-GO check before moving to Phase 2 (row counts, weights
         summing to 100% per fund per month, no blank identifiers)

  Result: fund_overlap_holdings_dataset.csv
          118,800 rows | 60 funds | 36 months | 350 unique stocks | 9 columns

PHASE 2 - OVERLAP ENGINE  (Day 4)
  Goal: turn the clean dataset into validated overlap and exposure numbers.
  Steps:
    2.1  Define pairwise overlap: for every stock two funds both hold, take
         the SMALLER of their two weights, then add those up
    2.2  Build the merged/blended portfolio view: apply an investor's
         allocation to each fund's holdings and combine by stock (ISIN)
    2.3  Build a tiny, hand-checked example (2 funds, 4 stocks) and confirm
         the code's answer matches a manual calculation exactly
    2.4  Scale the overlap calculation to every unique fund pair in one
         month (1,770 pairs for 60 funds) without becoming slow
    2.5  Repeat that calculation across all 36 months, so overlap can be
         tracked over time
    2.6  Sanity-check the results by fund category: e.g. two Large Cap
         funds should generally overlap much more than a Small Cap fund
         and an Index fund

PHASE 3 - APP AND SHIPPING  (Days 5-7)
  Goal: turn the verified analysis into a usable, deployed product.
  Steps:
    3.1  Build the app shell: choose funds, enter how much is invested in
         each, and choose a month
    3.2  Add a pairwise overlap heatmap for the selected funds
    3.3  Add a merged top-20 holdings view (real combined stock exposure)
    3.4  Add concentration flags: any stock above 5% of the total invested
         amount, and how many of the selected funds hold it
    3.5  Add a 36-month chart showing how overlap has moved over time
    3.6  Add an educational-use disclaimer and a synthetic-data label
    3.7  Deploy the app to a live web address and capture example findings

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW IT WAS BUILT (IMPLEMENTATION STEPS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Wrote the core calculation logic in Python (pandas/numpy):
       - overlap_core.py -- pairwise overlap, merged portfolio, category
         sanity checks, all-pairs and all-months calculations, data
         integrity checks
  2. Built the user interface with Streamlit (app.py), a Python framework
     that turns a script into an interactive web app with almost no
     front-end code.
  3. Verified every calculation against hand-worked examples before
     trusting it on the full dataset (Phase 2's fixture-check step).
  4. Cross-checked the app's output against the project's own reference
     material (worked examples in the course slide deck) to confirm the
     numbers matched exactly.
  5. Packaged the project for hosting:
       - requirements.txt (Python package versions needed)
       - README.md (what the app does and how to run it)
       - .streamlit/config.toml (basic app configuration)
  6. Pushed the code and dataset to a GitHub repository.
  7. Deployed the repository as a live app on Streamlit Community Cloud
     (share.streamlit.io), which rebuilds automatically whenever the
     GitHub repository is updated.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT THE APP DOES -- FOR A REAL USER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

When someone opens the live app, they see four tabs:

  TAB 1: APP (MAIN PRODUCT)
    - Search and select the funds you (or a hypothetical investor) hold.
    - Enter how much money is invested in each fund.
    - Pick a month-end date (defaults to the most recent one available).
    - See:
        * A colour-coded HEATMAP showing how much each pair of your
          selected funds overlaps with each other.
        * Your TOP 20 combined stock holdings -- what you actually own
          once all your funds are looked through and merged together.
        * A CONCENTRATION FLAG table: any single stock that makes up more
          than 5% of your total invested amount, and how many of your
          funds hold it.
        * A 3-YEAR TIME-SERIES CHART showing how the overlap between your
          chosen funds has moved over the last 36 months.
    - A reconciliation line confirms the numbers add up to exactly your
      total invested amount, so nothing is silently lost or duplicated.

  TAB 2: PAIRWISE OVERLAP
    - Pick any two funds directly and see their overlap percentage, how
      many stocks they share, and which shared stocks contribute the most
      to that overlap.

  TAB 3: ALL PAIRS & SANITY CHECK
    - See overlap for every possible fund pair in a chosen month at once.
    - Automatic checks confirm there are no errors in the calculation
      (no fund compared with itself, no duplicated pairs, no values
      outside the valid 0-100% range).
    - A category-level summary shows whether the overlap pattern makes
      sense (e.g. two Large Cap funds should typically overlap far more
      than a Small Cap fund and an Index fund).
    - Unusual pairs are flagged for review, not silently changed.

  TAB 4: DATA CHECKS
    - A transparency page showing that the underlying dataset itself is
      clean and trustworthy: holdings weights add up to 100% per fund
      per month, there are no missing values, no duplicate rows, and no
      negative weights.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. KEY RULES THE APP FOLLOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Securities are always matched by ISIN (a stock's unique identifier),
    never by company name, since the same company can be written multiple
    ways across different fund disclosures.
  - Cash and cash-equivalent holdings are excluded from all overlap and
    concentration maths -- only genuine stock holdings are compared.
  - Overlap between two funds = the sum, over every stock they both hold,
    of the SMALLER of their two weights in that stock.
  - Every number shown in the app is recalculated live from the raw
    dataset -- nothing is hard-coded or pre-computed and hidden.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. LIMITATIONS AND HONEST CAVEATS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - All funds, companies, and holdings in this dataset are FICTIONAL.
    Nothing here reflects a real fund, a real company, or real market data.
  - This is a point-in-time / historical snapshot tool. It does not use
    live prices and does not update in real time.
  - No output is investment advice. It does not recommend buying, selling,
    or holding anything.
  - The app is a coursework project. Some real-world features a production
    financial tool would need (user accounts, real disclosure ingestion,
    live data feeds) are intentionally out of scope.
